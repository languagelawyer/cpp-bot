### T47-T48 — our own reaction card read by the team's set, not by our sight (issues 1, 2)

- **Position:** stacks r3 y5 g5 b4 p2 i0 at T47. yagami's 3 to will-bot69 is a
  reactive with will-bot67 as the reacter: its o44, the i1, pairs with will-bot69's

    its own b5 and p3.
  - At T48 will-bot67 played o44. Its hole note read `r4,b5,p3,i1`, and will-bot69's
    o36 was called `[f] p4`.
- **Cause:**
  - `own_hole_play_reading` (`src/conventions/tiiah/superposition.cpp`) overrode the
    team's set only when it was a single identity our sight refutes. A wider set
    went into the hole unchanged.
  - `narrow_receiver_call` (`src/conventions/tiiah/interpret_reactive.cpp`) read the
    reacter's card from the team's set at the reacter's own seat. From the p3 it read
    the finesse, the p4.

- **Resolution (v23.36.0; tiiah `CONVENTION.md` §1d, "The reacter's card, at the
  reacter's own seat", and §1e, "The NOTE on our own hole card"):**
  - At the reacter's own seat its card is read as the team's set less every identity
    the seat can see is unplayed: every copy in a partner's hand or thrown on the
    wire (`tiiah::own_card_by_sight`). This applies in `narrow_receiver_call` and
    `receiver_world_fallback`.
  - Our own hole card's note shows that set too. The stored set stays the team's,
    the one partners predict from.
  - Not the seat's whole `possible`: that also rules out copies its own bookkeeping
    counts as played. Read that way, replay 2018874 T20 changed its reaction and
    lost the b5 its test pins at T47.
  - `build/replay_log.exe logs/will-bot67-2027736.log --turn 54 --notes`: o44

  - Test: `tests/test_tiiah/test_replay_2027736_receiver_call_read_through_own_sight.cpp`.
